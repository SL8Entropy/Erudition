5th Place Solution

Synthetic-Data-Centric CNN

ROGII - Wellbore Geology Prediction

Solution Writeup · 5th place · Aug 7, 2026

First of all, thanks to ROGII and Kaggle — this was a genuinely fun competition.
Special thanks to @hengck23 for generously sharing so many insights in the discussions — the base channel layout of my input images started from the representation he shared.

TL;DR: CNNs on TVT×MD images, pretrained for ~25 epochs on synthetic wells and fine-tuned for about 2 epochs on real wells; an ensemble of models with different backbones. To make my CV trustworthy, I focused above all on raising the fidelity of the synthetic data.

--
1. How I ended up centering everything on synthetic data
1.1. Particle filters

I started on July 4th and tried many approaches. Early on, my main line of work was characterizing the distribution of (horizontal GR − type well GR) and iterating on the likelihood function of a particle filter (PF). Adding PF variations kept improving the score. I had also started building synthetic data, and the CNN's accuracy was improving too. I tried combining PF with the synthetic data, but generating synthetic wells on the fly while computing multiple PF variants was too heavy, and I eventually dropped the PF entirely.
1.2. Synthetic data

The public notebooks showed a large gap between CV and public score. By training primarily on synthetic data, I aimed to make my CV trustworthy. I spent most of my time improving the fidelity of the synthetic data.
2. Building the synthetic data
2.1. Synthetic GR, version 1

The horizontal-well GR log is generated in this order:

    generate the type well GR
    generate the geological layers
    generate the drilling path
    generate the horizontal-well GR (type well GR plus noise)
    Neither the type well GR nor the horizontal GR copies fragments of real waveforms — everything is generated to be statistically similar. The horizontal-well GR is composed of three parts:

hwGR(md) = tw(tvt(md)) + δ(tvt(md), md) + ε(md)

    tw(tvt(md)): the type well GR value at the current TVT
    δ(tvt(md), md) (lateral heterogeneity): a Gaussian process over the TVT axis × (along MD: a static component + a slowly-swapping component with a long-correlation OU process)
    ε(md) (measurement noise): white noise + a short-wavelength OU + a long-wavelength OU
    The measurement noise reflects the fact that measurement errors are serially correlated; it is a function of MD. The lateral-heterogeneity term expresses how the residual pattern differs between wells: when a single well revisits the same TVT, a similar residual reappears, but the similarity fades as you move away along MD — δ captures that behavior.

2.2. Synthetic GR, version 2 (latent geology)

Looking at type well GR next to horizontal-well GR, you notice that the type well is smoothed along the TVT axis: GR variation from beds thinner than a certain thickness has been erased. Because of this, composing the horizontal GR as "type well plus noise" can never close the gap to real data.

So I switched to generating both the type well GR and the horizontal GR from a latent geology. The procedure becomes:

    generate the latent geological GR
    generate the type well GR
    generate the geological layers
    generate the drilling path
    generate the horizontal-well GR
    Both logs are now derived from the latent column:

tw(tvt)  = H_tw[L](tvt)
hwGR(md) = H_hw[L](tvt(md), md) + δ(tvt(md), md) + ε(md)
L = L_lo + L_hi     : latent GR split into low/high frequency bands

The latent geological GR is generated on a 0.25 ft grid. The type well GR is produced by smoothing the latent column and then sampling it at 0.5 ft, 1 ft, etc.
3. Input

Each horizontal well becomes a 256 × 768 (+48) image (rows = TVT bins, columns = 12 ft of MD each). The channels are:
# 	channel 	content
0 	type well GR 	each TVT row's type-well GR value, replicated across all columns (vertical stripes)
1 	hwGR 	each MD column's horizontal-well GR (12 ft column mean), replicated down all rows (horizontal stripes)
2 	GR coverage 	each column's fraction of valid GR samples (lower where data is missing), replicated down rows
3 	known path 	the confirmed pre-PS path drawn as a Gaussian line through its (TVT row, MD column) cells; blank after PS
4 	row coordinate 	each row's TVT relative to the anchor (PS-point TVT), replicated across columns (vertical gradient)
5 	cumulative −ΔZ 	cumulative Z change from the PS point to each column (where the path would sit under a flat-layer assumption)
6 	dip 	the trajectory's tangent slope per column (from dz/dmd, clipped)
7 	azimuth sin 	sine of the horizontal heading per column
8 	azimuth cos 	cosine component
9 	type well − hwGR misfit 	ch0 − ch1
10 	context type well GR 	reference type well of the ±800 ft neighborhood (each row's value replicated across columns)
11 	context coverage 	mask of rows where ch10's reference is valid
12 	median hw GR 	each column's GR aggregated by median instead of mean
13 	type well − median-hwGR misfit 	ch0 − ch12

A 256 × 48 image is concatenated on the right. It draws the type well GR and the pre-PS horizontal GR on TVT × GR axes.
4. Model: a CNN on the TVT × MD canvas

input 14ch × 256 rows × 768 cols (+48 appendix cols)
 ↓
① encoder:
  maxvit_tiny / effnetv2_rw_s / hrnet_w18
 ↓
② decoder: UNet-style
 ↓
③ heads
  head1: SDF = tanh × 3
  head2: row-classification logits

As an auxiliary loss, the model must paint the trajectory that the horizontal-well GR would trace if the true TVT path were followed — produced from the 256 × 48 image.

When you plot the horizontal-well GR on the TVT × GR chart using the correct path versus a wrong predicted path, the failure is sometimes visually obvious. I wanted the model to learn exactly that. This auxiliary loss improved the score by about 0.1 ft.

5. Training

Pretraining on synthetic data runs for 25 epochs. Fine-tuning on real data ran for 8 epochs, but the best epoch was almost always epoch 2. Augmentation on real data was just two things:

    shifting the PS position
    horizontal flip

6. Inference

    Type well correction: for wells sharing the same type well, I build a median residual profile from the sibling horizontal wells and add it to the type well. The type well GR serves as the expected GR value at each TVT; because of the type well's limited observation quality, there are places where it clearly disagrees with the horizontal GR. The other wells' horizontal GR values are used to update that expectation. I confirmed that most of the test data shares its type wells with the training data.
    Inference on a larger canvas:

    First predict every well on the standard canvas: 256 rows (0.5 ft/row, covering anchor ±64 ft).
    Edge detection: take each well's maximum |relative TVT| of the predicted path, and flag the well if it exceeds 55 ft.
    Re-infer flagged wells on a 384-row canvas (±96 ft) and replace their predictions entirely.

7. Validation strategy

The public LB is only ~50-60 wells and behaved like pure noise. My last six scored submissions, with CV, public and private:

Public and private are almost anti-correlated, while CV and private rank-correlate very well. My plan was to submit v11 — a 0.1 CV improvement over v5 on OOF — and select it as one of my final two. But it crashed with an error about 30 minutes before the deadline. I managed to resubmit 5 minutes before the deadline, and scoring completed 10 minutes after it. The score would have been 3rd place on private.
The cause: the img_size argument passed to timm at model construction is unnecessary for MaxViT-tiny but required for MaxViT-base.
8. What didn't work

    training with different seeds
    finer MD resolution

9. Distribution shift between train and test

A model trained only on synthetic data (no fine-tuning) scores 6.342 on private (≈15th place). Fine-tuning on real data improves OOF and public score by about 1.5 ft, but improves private by only about 0.5 ft. The training data and the public test data appear to be very similar, while the private test data seems to have a slightly different distribution. The type wells should be shared with the training data for most of the test set — perhaps the drillers or the GR tools differ. It looks like the competition ultimately tested robustness to this kind of small distribution shift.
model 	CV 	public 	private
synthetic pretraining only (no FT) 	6.250 	7.186 	6.342
with FT 	OOF 4.844 	5.600 	5.835
